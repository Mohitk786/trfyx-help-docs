Goals let you measure the actions that matter to your business or product. Instead of stopping at visits and page views, define outcomes such as a completed signup, a qualified lead, activation, or a purchase, then use those outcomes to evaluate product and marketing performance.

The exact goal configuration depends on the event-tracking options available in your Trfyx workspace.

## Choose a useful goal

A strong goal definition is specific, observable, and connected to an outcome you care about.

| Example goal | Definition to agree on |
| --- | --- |
| Signup completed | The account creation flow finished successfully. |
| Trial started | A user entered the trial state, not merely clicked the trial button. |
| Lead submitted | The form submission was accepted successfully. |
| Activation reached | The user completed a clearly defined first-value action. |
| Purchase completed | The payment or order was confirmed, not just initiated. |

Avoid ambiguous goals such as "engaged user" unless your team has defined exactly what engagement means.

## Configure and validate a goal

<Steps>
  <Step title="Agree on the business definition">
    Decide what counts as success, what should not count, and whether the goal is a one-time action or can occur multiple times.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose the event or supported tracking method">
    In Trfyx, configure the goal using the event or goal setup supported by your project. Follow the product's current setup guidance rather than guessing event names or payload formats.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Trigger the intended action">
    Use a test account to complete the full workflow. Confirm the event occurs at the right point, only after the outcome is real.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check counts and duplication">
    Verify that one successful action creates the expected number of recorded conversions. Check refreshes, retries, redirects, and repeated submissions for accidental duplicates.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Use the goal in analysis">
    Analyze conversion over time and across relevant audiences. Combine it with [funnels](/funnels), [user journeys](/user-journeys), or [revenue attribution](/revenue-attribution) depending on the question.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Goal quality checklist

- [ ] The goal has a clear business definition.
- [ ] It fires at the point the outcome is actually completed.
- [ ] Its event name and properties are consistent across environments.
- [ ] The implementation avoids accidental duplicate conversions.
- [ ] Test events and production events are distinguishable.
- [ ] The team understands the denominator when interpreting a conversion rate.

## Common issues

**The goal never fires.** Verify that the event is sent, that the trigger condition is reached, and that the data arrives in the correct Trfyx project.

**The goal count is too high.** Check duplicate tracking code, repeated event calls, page refreshes, and whether your definition allows multiple conversions per person or session.

**The conversion rate changed suddenly.** Review tracking releases, changes to the form or flow, acquisition mix, and the selected date range before attributing the difference to user behavior.

**The goal is firing before success.** Move the trigger to the confirmed success state rather than a button click or the beginning of a workflow.
