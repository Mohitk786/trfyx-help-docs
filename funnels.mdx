# Funnels

Funnels measure how people progress through a sequence of steps toward a defined outcome. They help answer not only how many people complete a workflow, but where the largest losses occur.

Typical funnels include landing page → signup → activation, pricing page → checkout → purchase, or onboarding started → setup completed → first meaningful action.

## Design a useful funnel

Before creating a funnel, define:

1. **The population:** Which visitors or users should be included?
2. **The entry point:** What event or step starts the flow?
3. **The completion point:** What counts as a successful outcome?
4. **The sequence:** Which intermediate steps matter for the question?
5. **The time window:** Over what period should users be allowed to progress?

Use events that represent meaningful progress. A click on a "Create account" button is not the same as a successfully created account.

## Build and read a funnel

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose the workflow">
    Select one high-value flow. Start with a short sequence so the result is easy to interpret.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Define each step">
    Use the available Trfyx event or page conditions to define the entry point, intermediate steps, and completion event. Keep definitions consistent when comparing time periods.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set relevant filters and time range">
    Limit the report to the audience and period relevant to your question. For comparisons, avoid changing several conditions at once.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Find the largest drop-off">
    Identify the stage where the biggest number or proportion of users fail to continue. Check whether the result is stable across different periods or segments before prioritizing it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Investigate the experience">
    Use [user journeys](/user-journeys) to explore alternate paths and [session replay](/session-replay) to look for friction in the relevant step.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Validate a change">
    Make one targeted improvement and compare the funnel after enough data has accumulated. Account for traffic mix and any tracking changes during the comparison.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Interpreting drop-off

A drop-off is a signal to investigate, not a complete explanation. Users may leave for many reasons: confusing copy, a technical error, an expected exit, poor traffic quality, or a step that is not required for every user.

Ask these questions before changing the product:

- Is the step instrumented correctly?
- Is the next step required for every user in this funnel?
- Does the drop-off differ by device, source, or audience?
- Do recordings or support reports reveal a consistent obstacle?
- Did a release or campaign change the mix of visitors?

## Common funnel mistakes

**Too many steps.** Begin with the core flow and add detail only when it helps diagnose a question.

**Inconsistent events.** Renamed or missing events can make historical comparisons unreliable.

**Confusing sequence with causality.** A step that correlates with conversion does not necessarily cause it.

**Comparing different audiences.** A funnel for high-intent returning visitors may behave differently from one for all first-time visitors.
