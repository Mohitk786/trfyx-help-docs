Revenue attribution answers the question every founder and marketer asks: **"Where is my revenue actually coming from?"**

When someone lands on your site from a Google ad, browses three pages, leaves, comes back a week later from an email campaign, and finally buys your product — who gets the credit? Revenue attribution in Trfyx connects the dots between that visitor's journey and the payment that came through your Stripe, Paddle, or any other provider.

<Note>
  Revenue attribution is available on the **Growth** plan and above. You'll need the Trfyx tracker installed on your site and at least one payment provider connected.
</Note>

## How it works, in plain English

Here's what happens behind the scenes when a customer pays you:

1. **Your payment provider sends a webhook** to Trfyx the moment a payment goes through (a checkout, a subscription renewal, a refund — all of it).
2. **Trfyx figures out which visitor made that payment.** This is the hard part. We use a [6-step cascading match](#how-trfyx-matches-payments-to-visitors) that tries progressively broader methods — from an exact session match all the way down to an email lookup.
3. **We look up that visitor's acquisition history.** What was the first page they ever visited? What UTM campaign brought them? What was the last thing they did before paying?
4. **We record both first-touch and last-touch attribution** so you can compare "what channel introduced this customer" vs. "what channel closed the deal."
5. **If the payment is in a foreign currency**, we automatically convert it to your reporting currency using live exchange rates so your totals make sense.

The result: every payment in your dashboard shows which source, campaign, medium, and landing page gets credit — with a confidence level so you know how reliable each match is.

## Quick setup (5 minutes)

<Steps>
  <Step title="Install the Trfyx tracker">
    If you haven't already, add the Trfyx tracking script to your website. This is what captures visitor sessions, UTM parameters, referrers, and landing pages — the raw material for attribution.

    See [Install tracking](/install-tracking) for your framework.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect a payment provider">
    Go to **Site Settings → Payments** in your dashboard, pick your provider, and paste your API key. Trfyx creates the webhook automatically — you don't need to configure anything on the provider side.

    We support **7 providers**: [Stripe](/revenue-attribution/setup-stripe), [LemonSqueezy](/revenue-attribution/setup-lemonsqueezy), [Polar](/revenue-attribution/setup-polar), [Paddle](/revenue-attribution/setup-paddle), [Dodo Payments](/revenue-attribution/setup-dodo), [Yolfi](/revenue-attribution/setup-yolfi), and [Shopify](/shopify).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set your reporting currency">
    In **Site Settings → Payments**, choose the currency you want all revenue reported in (e.g. USD, EUR, GBP). Payments in other currencies are automatically converted using live exchange rates.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Make a test purchase">
    Use your provider's test mode to create a payment. Within a few seconds, it should appear in **Revenue → Overview** in your dashboard. Check that the amount, source, and match method look correct.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tag your campaigns">
    Use UTM parameters on your marketing links (`utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`). These are how Trfyx knows which campaign or ad brought a visitor. Without them, traffic shows up as "Direct" or by referrer domain only.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## How Trfyx matches payments to visitors

This is the core of the attribution engine. When a payment webhook arrives, Trfyx runs through **6 matching steps in order**, stopping at the first one that finds a visitor. Each step has a confidence level:

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Step 1: Explicit session (High confidence)" icon="bullseye">
    **What it checks:** Did the payment provider include a visitor or session ID in its metadata?

    **When it works:** If you pass a Trfyx visitor ID in your checkout metadata (e.g. Stripe's `metadata` field), this is the most reliable match possible. The payment is directly tied to the session that initiated the checkout.

    **Confidence:** High — there's no guessing involved.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Step 2: Checkout URL detection (High confidence)" icon="link">
    **What it checks:** Did the Trfyx tracker detect the visitor returning to your site from a checkout page?

    **When it works:** Many payment providers redirect the customer back to your site after checkout (like Stripe Checkout's `success_url`). The Trfyx tracker notices these return URLs and records which visitor landed on them. When the webhook arrives, we match it to that detection.

    **Confidence:** High — we saw the same visitor on the checkout return page.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Step 3: Subscription history (High confidence)" icon="rotate">
    **What it checks:** For renewal payments, is there an earlier payment from the same subscription that was already attributed?

    **When it works:** If a customer's first subscription payment was attributed to a visitor, all future renewals for that same subscription automatically inherit that attribution. This means your MRR stays connected to the original acquisition channel.

    **Confidence:** High — the subscription ID is an exact match.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Step 4: Customer identity (Medium confidence)" icon="user">
    **What it checks:** Has this customer (by provider customer ID or email) been linked to a visitor before?

    **When it works:** Trfyx builds a customer identity graph over time. If we've previously linked a Stripe customer `cus_abc123` to visitor `v_xyz`, any new payment from that customer automatically connects to that visitor.

    **Confidence:** Medium — the customer is the same, but they might be using a different device or browser than the one we tracked.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Step 5: Provider customer history (Medium confidence)" icon="clock-rotate-left">
    **What it checks:** Has the same provider customer ID made any previous payment that was attributed?

    **When it works:** Similar to Step 4, but looks directly at previous revenue events rather than the identity graph. Useful as a fallback when the identity system hasn't built a link yet.

    **Confidence:** Medium.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Step 6: Email fallback (Low confidence)" icon="envelope">
    **What it checks:** Has the customer's email address appeared in any tracked events in ClickHouse?

    **When it works:** This is the last resort. If the customer's email (from the payment) matches an email captured in analytics events, we use that visitor. It's better than no match, but emails can be shared across devices or people.

    **Confidence:** Low — an email match is less reliable than a session or customer ID match.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

If none of the 6 steps find a match, the payment is recorded as **unattributed**. You can see unattributed payments in the [Diagnostics tab](#diagnostics-tab) and investigate why they weren't matched.

## Understanding the Revenue dashboard

Once payments start flowing in, your Revenue section has **5 tabs**:

### Overview tab

Your at-a-glance revenue picture. Here's what each number means:

| Metric | What it shows | How it's calculated |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Total Revenue** | All payments received in the selected date range | Sum of all `RevenueEvent` amounts, converted to your reporting currency |
| **Conversions** | Number of individual payment events | Count of payment webhooks received (checkout completions, subscription payments, etc.) |
| **Avg Order Value** | Average revenue per conversion | Total revenue ÷ number of conversions |
| **Attribution Rate** | What percentage of payments were matched to a visitor | Attributed conversions ÷ total conversions × 100 |

Below the KPIs you'll find:

- **Revenue over time** — a daily chart showing total and attributed revenue
- **Revenue by source** — which traffic sources (Google, Twitter, direct, etc.) drove the most revenue, shown for both first-touch and last-touch
- **Revenue by campaign** — same breakdown but by UTM campaign
- **Revenue by landing page** — which pages visitors first landed on before eventually paying
- **Recent payments** — a live feed of your latest payment events with match method and provider

### Attribution tab

A deep-dive comparison of **first-touch vs. last-touch revenue** across four dimensions:

- **Source** — the traffic source (google, twitter, newsletter, direct)
- **Campaign** — the UTM campaign name
- **Landing page** — the first page the visitor saw
- **Medium** — the traffic medium (organic, paid, email, social)

For each row, you see:

- **First-touch revenue** — revenue credited to the channel that _first introduced_ the customer
- **Last-touch revenue** — revenue credited to the channel of the visitor's _most recent session_ before paying
- **Conversions** — how many payments came through this dimension
- **AOV** — average order value for this dimension

This tab also shows **unattributed payments** — payments that couldn't be matched to any visitor. If you see a lot of these, check the [Diagnostics tab](#diagnostics-tab).

### Customers tab

A list of all identified customers across all your payment providers. Each row shows:

- Customer name and email (from the payment provider)
- Total lifetime revenue
- Number of payments
- First-touch acquisition source
- Date of last payment
- Subscription status (if applicable)

Click any customer to see their full detail page: payment history, linked visitor sessions, and how they were matched.

### Subscriptions tab

Tracks your recurring revenue in real time:

| Metric | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **MRR** | Monthly Recurring Revenue — the sum of all active subscriptions normalized to monthly. Annual plans are divided by 12; weekly plans are multiplied by 4.33. |
| **Active** | Subscriptions currently in `active` status |
| **Trialing** | Subscriptions in a free trial period |
| **Churned** | Subscriptions canceled or expired in the selected date range |

Below the KPIs:

- **MRR by plan** — a bar chart showing how much MRR comes from each plan
- **Active subscriptions** — a table with plan name, billing interval, MRR contribution, and next renewal date
- **Recent churns** — subscriptions that canceled recently, with the cancel reason if the provider shared one

### Diagnostics tab

Your attribution health dashboard. Use this when something looks off:

- **Attribution rate** — the percentage of payments that were successfully matched to a visitor. A green dot means ≥ 80%, yellow means 50–80%, red means \< 50%.
- **Match method distribution** — shows how many payments were matched by each of the 6 cascade steps. If most matches are "email_fallback," consider passing visitor IDs in your checkout metadata for higher confidence.
- **Confidence distribution** — breaks down matches by confidence level (high / medium / low / none).
- **Re-attribute button** — re-runs the entire cascade engine across all your payments. Useful after you've fixed tracking issues or connected a new identity source.

## Currency conversion

Trfyx automatically converts all payment amounts to your chosen **reporting currency** so your totals are consistent, even if customers pay in different currencies.

**How it works:**

- Exchange rates are fetched from a free, open-source currency API
- Rates are cached for 1 hour, so slight fluctuations within the hour won't cause inconsistencies
- Both the original amount/currency and the converted amount are stored — you never lose the raw data
- If a rate isn't available for an exotic currency, the original amount is used as-is

**To set your reporting currency:** Go to **Site Settings → Payments** and choose your currency. This affects all future conversions and the display currency in the Revenue dashboard.

## First-touch vs. last-touch: which should you use?

Both are shown side by side because they answer different questions:

| Model | Question it answers | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **First-touch** | "What channel _introduced_ this customer to us?" | Evaluating top-of-funnel: awareness campaigns, SEO, content marketing |
| **Last-touch** | "What was the last thing the customer interacted with before paying?" | Evaluating bottom-of-funnel: retargeting, email sequences, special offers |

**Example:** A customer first visits from a blog post (organic search), then comes back from a retargeting ad, then converts from an email campaign. First-touch credits organic search. Last-touch credits email. Both are valid — they just measure different things.

<Tip>
  If first-touch and last-touch show very different numbers for a channel, that channel might be great at introducing customers but poor at closing them (or vice versa). That's a useful insight.
</Tip>

## Confidence levels explained

Every payment match comes with a confidence level. Here's what they mean in practice:

| Level | What it means | Typical match methods |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **High** | Near-certain match. The visitor and the payment are directly linked. | Explicit session, checkout URL detection, subscription history |
| **Medium** | Strong match, but relies on an identity link that could theoretically be wrong (e.g., same customer on a different device). | Customer identity, provider customer history |
| **Low** | Best-effort match. The link is indirect. | Email fallback |
| **None** | No match found. The payment is unattributed. | — |

A high attribution rate with mostly "high" confidence matches means your tracking setup is solid. If you see lots of "low" or "none," see [Improving your attribution rate](#improving-your-attribution-rate).

## Improving your attribution rate

If your attribution rate is below 80%, here are the most common fixes:

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Pass visitor IDs in checkout metadata" icon="fingerprint">
    The single most impactful thing you can do. When creating a Stripe Checkout session (or equivalent), include the Trfyx visitor ID in the metadata. This gives you Step 1 (explicit session) matches with high confidence.

    Most payment providers support metadata or custom fields on checkouts. Check your provider's API docs.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Make sure the tracker is on your checkout success page" icon="check">
    After a customer completes a purchase, they usually land on a "thank you" or confirmation page. If the Trfyx tracker is loaded on that page, it automatically detects the checkout return URL and creates a high-confidence match (Step 2).

    If your success page is on a different domain or subdomain, make sure it also has the tracker installed.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Use consistent UTM parameters" icon="tags">
    UTMs don't directly affect _whether_ a payment is attributed, but they determine _what_ it's attributed to. Without UTMs, attributed payments show up under generic sources like "direct" or a referrer domain.

    Use a consistent naming convention: `utm_source=google&utm_medium=cpc&utm_campaign=spring-sale`
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Check for ad blockers and tracking prevention" icon="shield">
    Some visitors block the Trfyx tracker, which means there's no session to match against. Consider using a [proxy setup](/proxy-guide) to route tracking through your own domain — this significantly reduces blocking.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Ensure webhook delivery" icon="webhook">
    If payments aren't showing up at all, the webhook might be failing. Check your payment provider's webhook logs for delivery errors. The webhook URL should be `https://your-app-domain/api/webhooks/revenue/YOUR_WEBSITE_ID`.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## What counts as a "conversion"

In the Revenue dashboard, a **conversion** is any payment event received from your provider. This includes:

- **Checkout completions** — one-time purchases
- **Subscription payments** — initial subscription sign-ups
- **Subscription renewals** — recurring charges on existing subscriptions
- **Invoice payments** — invoices marked as paid

It does **not** count:

- Refunds (these are tracked separately and marked on the original payment)
- Failed payments
- Disputes (tracked separately with their own status)
- Test-mode transactions (if your provider is in test mode, these still appear — switch to live mode before going to production)

---

## Refunds, disputes, and cancellations

Trfyx tracks the full lifecycle of a payment, not just the initial charge:

- **Refunds** — when a charge is refunded, the original payment is marked as refunded. Refunded amounts are not subtracted from revenue totals by default (the original payment still shows in attribution reports with a "refunded" badge).
- **Disputes** — if a customer disputes a charge, Trfyx records the dispute with its status (open, won, lost, withdrawn). Dispute resolution events are also tracked.
- **Cancellations** — subscription cancellations are recorded with the cancel reason (if the provider shares one). The subscription's MRR contribution drops to zero.
